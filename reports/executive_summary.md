# Online Retail II — Executive Summary

*A plain-English summary of the full analysis. For the detailed version with charts and methods, see [final_report.md](final_report.md).*

## The one thing to take away

A small group of existing customers drives almost all the revenue, and the clearest growth opportunity abroad is reaching more people — not fixing how they shop. Marketing effort is best spent keeping high-value customers loyal, turning first-time buyers into repeat buyers, and getting in front of more overseas shoppers.

## What we found

- **Revenue is highly concentrated.** The top ~40% of identifiable customers (about 1,800 people) bring in roughly 85% of revenue. The lowest-value 14% bring in just over 1%. The single top customer alone accounts for £280,000.
- **Repeat customers are worth about 7x more.** A repeat customer is worth roughly £2,900 on average; a one-time buyer about £410. Around two-thirds of identifiable customers have bought more than once.
- **The second purchase is the sticking point.** Of customers acquired in a given month, only about 15–25% buy again the following month. After that, the ones who stick around keep buying at a steady rate — so the hard part is getting people from their first order to their second.
- **Overseas customers punch above their weight.** They're about 10% of the customer base but nearly 20% of revenue. In every country with enough data to judge (Germany, France, Switzerland, Belgium, Spain), they buy at least as often as UK customers and spend as much or more per order.
- **Demand builds toward November.** Order volume climbs steadily through autumn and peaks in November, ahead of the holiday season. (The data stops on 9 December, so the apparent December drop is just missing days, not a real decline.)
- **Returns are steady except January.** About 8% of order value is returned across the year — normal for retail — but January runs more than double that, likely post-holiday returns.

## What this means for marketing

Because revenue sits with so few customers, losing a handful of top spenders costs more than winning many small ones — retention of the best customers matters more than broad acquisition. The overseas gap is not a product, pricing, or delivery problem: those customers already behave well once they buy. The issue is simply that too few of them ever find the store. And across the whole base, the most common missed opportunity is the customer who buys once and is never contacted again.

## Recommended actions

1. **Protect the top tier first (highest priority).** Build a recognisable loyalty or VIP programme for the ~1,800 top customers — early access to new stock, rewards tied to their own order history. It's inexpensive to run and the most damaging thing to neglect.
2. **Win the second order.** Target one-time and occasional buyers 2–6 weeks after their purchase with a time-limited "come back" offer. This is exactly where customers are lost, and a small nudge at the right moment is cheaper than re-acquiring them later.
3. **Put acquisition budget into the countries that already convert.** Focus paid search, paid social, and marketplace presence on Germany, France, Switzerland, and Belgium. The demand there converts well — it just needs more people in the funnel.
4. **Plan the Q4 push around November, not December.** Front-load campaigns, email, and stock so the peak lands in October–November when customers are actually buying.
